Not decided yet. Jay is still thinking it over (Q95b, Q96, Q26). Today's manor is DESIGN.md 7.6.

## What Jay said

- "there should be a spire manor on everyone floor. thoughts?"
- "i like all the features but i dont feel like they are honestly enough. the waystone feature under that currently logic is a little pointless. the spire manor already exists within the relatively small city, so its not much an advantage to teleport from the depot to spire manor. the features should feel more premium."
- "no matter the direction, i want to try to avoid the player having buys remorse for the spire manor. it should be worth the money and loaded with features."
- Examples from other servers he played (examples only; they break some of our rules):
  1. Private boss summoning in the house: normal boss cooldowns, but a guaranteed fight and no loot stolen by other players.
  2. House-only monsters on the first floor that respawned hourly and gave the best experience in the game (a share of the experience bar per kill). Owners sold kills and spawn times to other players, so it wasn't only for donors.
  3. Six house portals the owner points anywhere; two chances to change them, then locked for good.
  4. A casino: NPCs running dice, roulette and other games, with live hosts taking item bets (double, triple, cash over or under value); what the NPCs won went to the house owner.

## Claude's view

- **A manor on every floor: yes.** Ten buyers a season instead of one; each manor goes on sale the day its floor opens (an event each time); each themed to its floor and grander as you climb; one manor per account. Floors 2-10's manors are built with their floors (Floor 1 first).
- **Open House** (explained to Jay): the owner opens the house to the public for a set time and it is listed on a board in town (ordinary houses 2 hours once a day, the manor 6 hours twice a day).
- **Replace the Waystone with Jay's portal room:** 6 portals the owner points anywhere on the manor's floor (hunting ground gates, a favourite deep spot); 2 changes per season, then locked; invited guests can use them; never into boss rooms or secrets.

### Three levels of what money can buy

The rulebook's pillar is "never pay-to-win" (DESIGN 1, 11).

**Level 1: prestige and comfort, no power.** Today's features, plus: the 6 portals; a statue of the owner in the town square and their name on the website; a banner in their colours; an exclusive outfit and aura each season; unlimited mannequins; a full kitchen (cooking design); guest rooms rented out to other players for gold.

**Level 2: time and certainty, never a stronger character (Claude's recommendation, with Level 1).**
- Private boss room (Jay's example 1): the floor's bosses on the normal cooldowns; guaranteed fight, no stolen kill; normal loot and experience; no boss sigil needed in the manor.
- Private hunting preserve: a walled ground only the owner and guests use, so the spawn is never taken; **normal** experience, about a good public ground's.
- The owner can sell access to the preserve, the boss room and the portals for gold (the good part of example 2): others can buy in, and the owner earns much of the price back in game. The best answer to buyer's remorse.
- A 10-person guest list sharing the manor's comforts.

**Level 3: real power (Claude recommends no).**
- The experience monsters (example 2): money makes characters stronger, the pay-to-win that empties servers. Level 2's preserve keeps the good parts without the extra experience.
- The NPC casino paying the owner (example 4): a real-money buyer earning gold from a gambling house, while gold can be bought indirectly through the shop, looks like running a real-money casino and risks payment companies and the law (same worry as Q56's Fortune Hall). Keep player-hosted gambling; decide anything bigger after professional advice (roadmap 7.5).

**Borderline, Jay's call:** a manor bed that refills stamina faster (comfort, but slightly more full-experience time).
