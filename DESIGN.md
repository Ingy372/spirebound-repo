## 1. What Spirebound is

An Open Tibia server shaped like a floating tower. Everyone starts on Floor 1. Each floor ends in a labyrinth with a boss room. The first time anyone kills a floor boss, the next floor opens for the whole server with a server-wide announcement. Players pick a difficulty when they create a character, all the way up to **Death Game**, where dying deletes the character.

It plays like Tibia (hunting, loot, skulls, houses, guilds, depots, parties, the four familiar vocations, gold/platinum/crystal coins) with SAO-style touches: a floor indicator, sword skills on a hotbar, green/orange/red player cursors, an Info Broker who buys secrets, and a hidden dungeon under Floor 1 that grows as the tower opens.

### Pillars (what every decision is checked against)
1. **The tower is shared.** Floors open for everyone. The server has one story, written by its players.
2. **PvP with teeth, not bullying.** Fights are real, but the game actively protects weaker and newer players. Toxicity is why Jay has quit servers, so this is the top priority.
3. **Exploration pays.** Secrets are everywhere and worth money, bragging rights, or both.
4. **Houses do things.** Houses are functional: portals, private boss rooms, gambling halls, gardens.
5. **Never pay-to-win.** The best gear and spells come only from playing.
6. **Plain to learn.** Every quest says what to do next. No hour-long puzzles you can't figure out (Jay's own pain point).

### Naming rule
Original names inspired by SAO. No SAO character, place, item, or skill names anywhere a player can see. `tools/banned_names.txt` + `tools/check_names.py` enforce this on every build. Same for CipSoft monster/NPC/city names.

### Art and assets
- Canary and OTClient 15.11 read Tibia's modern asset format (`appearances.dat` + sprite sheets).
- **Placeholders:** base Tibia sprites from the official 15.11 client files, as Jay asked. The setup script downloads them onto Jay's PC. **They are never committed to GitHub and never shipped to the public** (CipSoft owns them). Each can be turned back on later as a design decision. Weapon proficiency (new in 15.11) stays **on**, because sword skills hook into it.

## 3. The tower

### 3.1 Floors
- 10 floors at launch. More added in updates.
- Each floor: a town (Floor 1 has two), hunting grounds, one "apex" spawn (see 3.5), a **field boss** guarding the only pass, a **labyrinth** behind the pass, and the **floor boss** room at the top of the labyrinth.
- Floor 1 is open at launch. Every other floor is sealed until its unlock.

| Floor | Name | Theme | Level band (hunting target) | Floor boss | **(Claude's call)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hearthgate Plains | meadows, ruins, windmills, river | 1–35 | Axe-Lord of the Stair | names and bands |
| 2 | Highrest Highlands | cliffs, herds, bone mesas | 35–60 | Crowned Aurochs | |
| 3 | Rootwell Fogwood | fog forest, giant roots | 60–90 | Mossback Warden | |
| 4 | Lakehold | lake castle, islands, boats | 90–120 | Tidewrought Castellan | |
| 5 | Ringhold | arena city, colosseum ruins | 120–160 | The Undying Champion | |
| 6 | Emberreach | volcanic badlands, forges | 160–200 | Cinder Matriarch | |
| 7 | Glasswater Marsh | swamp, crystal reeds | 200–250 | The Many-Mouthed | |
| 8 | Frostspire | snow peaks, ice caves | 250–300 | Rimebound Jarl | |
| 9 | The Hollow Bazaar | ruined merchant city, catacombs | 300–350 | The Ledger King | |
| 10 | The Cobalt Labyrinth | blue-lit stone maze | 350–400+ | **Azhrakkar, the Cerulean Ruin** (see 9.3) | |

Level bands are hunting targets, not doors. No level cap.

### 3.2 How a floor unlocks
1. The **field boss** at the pass must die once (anyone, any time). After that the labyrinth door is open forever for everyone.
2. The **floor boss** room is at the end of the labyrinth. Up to 20 players inside. PvP is on inside boss rooms.
3. **First kill of a floor boss:**
   - opens the stair and the teleport gate to the next floor for everyone;
   - plays a server-wide gold announcement + bell: *"Floor 3 has been cleared by <names>! The way to Floor 4 is open."*;
   - opens the next wing of the Floor 1 hidden dungeon (3.4);
   - starts the house head-start window for the new floor's town (7.4);
   - carves the credited names on the Monument in Hearthgate (and later on the website).
4. **Credit:** anyone who damaged the boss, or healed someone who did, in the last 90 seconds. Top 20 by contribution if more qualify. Credited players get a floor title ("Gatebreaker", "Hornbreaker", …), a cosmetic weapon glow, and the first-kill loot. The **last hit** gets the title "Last Blow (Floor N)" and a bound Last Blow Shard (an upgrade material, not a weapon or stats). *(From the old pack.)*
5. **Enrage scales with headcount** so a stuck server can still be unstuck by a small group: enrage timer = 8 min × 8 ÷ players (max 60 min). Solo = 60 min, 4 players = 16 min, 8+ = 8 min. *(From the old pack.)*
6. **Repeat kills:** after the first kill the boss becomes repeatable from a portal beside the stair for parties of 1–5, once per character per 20 hours **(Claude's call)**.

### 3.3 Towns and safe zones
- Hearthgate (Floor 1) is our echo of SAO's starting city: same feeling (big temple plaza, where everyone begins and returns), original name and layout.
- **Safe zones are in towns only** (Jay's rule). Inside towns, depots and houses nobody can attack anyone. Everywhere else PvP is possible, with the protections in section 6.

### 3.4 The Floor 1 hidden dungeon: the Underkeep
- A dark stone dungeon under Hearthgate. Its entrance is itself a secret (drain grate by the river).
- One map that grows. A new wing opens each time **two** floors are cleared **(Claude's call)**: Wing 1 at launch (once found), Wing 2 when Floor 2 opens, Wing 3 at Floor 4, Wing 4 at Floor 6, Wing 5 at Floor 8, Wing 6 at Floor 10. Later seasons add more wings.
- Each wing is tuned to the floor that opened it, not to the player. A level 10 in Wing 5 should leave fast.
- Every wing hides at least one more secret (shop, boss, chest room, fishing pool).
- Sealed doors read: *"Sealed until the tower reaches Floor N."*

### 3.5 Apex spawns (from Jay's favorite hunt)
One layered, over-tuned spawn per floor that stays worth hunting long past its floor: an easy outer layer, a packed inner room, a semi-hidden rope spot to a big deeper spawn, and a hidden side dungeon with easier, denser monsters. Apex spawns sit in **Contested** PvP zones (6.3), so they create the fights Jay remembers.

### 3.6 Something always happening
Every 3 hours a world event fires on an open floor: Frenzy (apex spawn +50% respawn, +25% loot), Incursion (elite packs), Fishing Frenzy (rare-fish rate up), Wandering Champion (roaming mini-boss). Announced 10 minutes ahead. *(From the old pack, fishing event added.)*

## 4. Seasons

Jay's brief: 20–30 floors per season with seasonal resets; account-wide rewards carry across seasons. Recommend 100 permanent floors instead only if analysis says seasons won't hold a player base.

### Analysis and recommendation: **keep seasons.**
- Seasonal resets are a proven OT model. Cyntara (the server Jay remembers best) runs on seasons and is on its 19th version; its launch week is what Jay remembers most fondly. Every reset is a new "launch week" with a fresh race, which is the strongest population spike an OT server gets.
- Seasons directly fix the toxicity problem Jay cares about most: on permanent servers the level gap between veterans and newcomers grows forever, and that gap is what lets top guilds farm new players. A reset closes the gap every few months.
- 100 permanent floors would need roughly 4–10× the content before the server feels "complete", and the world-first race would happen only once per floor, ever.
- **Jay confirmed seasons on 2026-11-08** ("Seasons are a go").

### Season rules **(Claude's call, Jay can veto)**
- Season length: about **5 months**. Season 1 launches with 10 floors; content updates add floors through the season, target **25 floors** by season end (inside Jay's 20–30 range).
- Floors are built once and **remixed** in later seasons (new bosses mechanics, shuffled secrets, new Underkeep wings), so content keeps paying off.
- **Resets:** characters, levels, items, gold, houses, guilds' houses, and secret discoveries.
- **Carries over (account-wide):** titles, cosmetic outfits and mounts, Death Game titles, fishing collection log, explorer titles, donation-shop purchases (cosmetics), Hall of Fame entries (first clears, Death Game leaderboard).
- Old-season characters are frozen in a read-only "Hall of Seasons" on the website (name, level, achievements).
- Final two weeks of a season: "Last Stand" events (double XP weekends, the season's final boss rushes), announced well ahead.

## 5. Characters

### 5.1 Creating a character
Account is created on the website (or, while testing locally, by a script). A new character wakes in the **Arrival Yard** in Hearthgate with no vocation. A short guided path (under 10 minutes) teaches movement, combat, looting and the skill bar, and ends at the Registrar NPC, where the player picks:
1. **Vocation** (5.2)
2. **Difficulty mode** (5.4)
(Choosing in-game rather than on a website form works without a website and makes the choice feel like a moment. **Claude's call, Jay can veto.**)

### 5.2 Vocations (four, familiar to Tibia players)

| Spirebound name | Tibia equivalent | Weapon families (each has its own sword skills) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vanguard** | Knight | Sword, Axe, Club (+ shield) | Tank, front line |
| **Ranger** | Paladin | Bow, Crossbow, Spear (throwing) | Ranged damage, some healing |
| **Arcanist** | Sorcerer | Wand ("spellblade" arts) | Area damage, big flashy spells |
| **Mystic** | Druid | Rod ("spellblade" arts) | Healer, support, nature damage |

Names are **(Claude's call, Jay can veto)**. Vocations keep Tibia's familiar feel: hit points, mana, capacity, skills (sword/axe/club/distance/shielding/magic level/fishing), and promotion at level 20 (Vanguard → Blade Warden, Ranger → Far Warden, Arcanist → High Arcanist, Mystic → Elder Mystic) for the familiar regeneration bump **(Claude's call)**.

Every vocation unlocks about 20 spells, including utility and support spells (Jay), listed in 5.3b. Melee and ranged attack power comes mostly from **sword skills**; Arcanists and Mystics also get big attack spells.

### 5.3 Sword skills
Every vocation gets sword skills: combo-style abilities with cooldowns, tied to the weapon type in hand. Change weapon type, change skill list.
- **Hotbar:** a dedicated sword-skill bar in our client (up to 6 slots), separate from normal hotkeys. Skills glow when ready and show their cooldown.
- **Unlocks:** each weapon family has 6 skills. 2 at the start, then more unlock through weapon proficiency (Canary 15.11's built-in proficiency per weapon) and through quests/secrets **(Claude's call)**.
- **Combos:** every skill is tagged *Opener*, *Link* or *Finisher*. Opener → Link → Finisher within 4 seconds of each other is a **combo**: the Finisher deals +30% and the combo counter on the bar lights up. Skills still work alone; combos are a skill reward, not a requirement **(Claude's call)**.
- **Cooldowns** are enforced by the server (a bot gains nothing from fast clicking). Each skill has its own cooldown plus a short shared 1-second "skill" cooldown.
- **Look:** sword skills must be "captivating to watch" (Jay). Each gets a distinct effect; big ones get bigger art budgets in `ART_REQUESTS.md`.
- First skill set (examples; full list lives in the skill data file, `server/.../sword_skills.lua`):
  - Sword: *Arc Slash* (Opener, front 3 tiles), *Riposte Line* (Link, line of 3), *Fourfold Rend* (Finisher, 4 rapid hits on one target)
  - Axe: *Split Earth* (Opener, area around you), *Hewing Turn* (Link), *Executioner's Drop* (Finisher, +damage vs targets under 30%)
  - Club: *Bell Ringer* (Opener, 1 s stun on monsters), *Crushing Arc* (Link), *Tremor Blow* (Finisher, area)
  - Bow: *Quickdraw* (Opener), *Pinning Shot* (Link, slow), *Arrow Storm* (Finisher, area)
  - Crossbow: *Bolt Lance* (Opener, line), *Ratchet Shot* (Link), *Siege Bolt* (Finisher)
  - Spear: *Skewer* (Opener), *Javelin Arc* (Link), *Comet Spear* (Finisher)
  - Wand (Arcanist): *Spark Edge* (Opener), *Flame Script* (Link, area), *Starfall Rite* (Finisher, big area)
  - Rod (Mystic): *Thorn Edge* (Opener), *Frost Script* (Link, slow), *Verdant Burst* (Finisher, damage + small party heal)

### 5.4 Difficulty modes
Chosen at creation. You can move **down** a difficulty at the Registrar (Death Game → Hardcore → Normal), **never up**.

| | Normal | Hardcore | Death Game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Death | Standard Tibia death penalty | Heavier penalty (below) | **Character is deleted** (retired to the Memorial) |
| XP bonus | — | +25% | +50% |
| Loot bonus | — | +15% | +30% |
| Cursor tag | none | small flame icon | skull-and-blade icon |
| Exclusive | — | Hardcore title | Death Game title, exclusive outfit, own leaderboard |

Bonus percentages are **(Claude's call, Jay can veto)**.

**Normal = standard Tibia:** you lose a share of your experience and skill progress on death, reduced by blessings bought from the temple priest; with no blessings and no amulet you can drop items. Red/black skulls lose more (6.4).

**Hardcore (Claude's call):** experience and skill loss ×1.5. Blessings can only reduce the penalty by half (not fully). Item drop chance on death doubled. No amulet that prevents item loss.

**Death Game (Claude's call on details):**
- Death by monsters, bosses, traps or environment deletes the character. In practice: the character is **retired**, locked forever, and placed on the Memorial wall in Hearthgate and the website with level, floor reached and cause of death. The account keeps every title and cosmetic already earned.
- Exclusive rewards: title "Deathbound" on reaching level 50, the exclusive Death Game outfit at level 100 (outfit addons at 200/300), leaderboard by level and floors cleared. All are account-wide and carry across seasons.
- **Moving down:** a Death Game character can step down to Hardcore or Normal any time in town. It keeps cosmetics already earned, loses its leaderboard spot (listed as "stepped down at level X"), and can never return to Death Game.
- **Player killers can never delete a Death Game character.** See 6.6.

### 5.5 Experience, stages and money
- Medium rates with stages (faster early, slower later), **no level cap**. Starting stages **(Claude's call, tuned after playtests)**:

| Level | XP rate |
|---|---|
| 1–50 | 25× |
| 51–100 | 15× |
| 101–150 | 10× |
| 151–200 | 7× |
| 201–300 | 5× |
| 301–400 | 3× |
| 401+ | 2× |

- Skill rate 10×, magic level 5×, loot rate 2×, fishing 5× **(Claude's call)**.
- **Currency:** Tibia's gold, platinum and crystal coins (100 gold = 1 platinum, 100 platinum = 1 crystal). Bank in every town.
- **Stamina:** standard Tibia stamina (full XP for the first hours of a day's play, reduced after) **(Claude's call, it also limits botting; Cyntara uses a similar system)**.

### 5.6 Dual wielding: the rare unique skill "Twin Fang"
- Earned in-game only. Never sold.
- Hidden trial on Floor 8 (itself a secret). To enter: Vanguard, sword proficiency at a high tier, and you must have finished a sword-skill combo 1,000 times **(Claude's call)**.
- **Rare:** only the first **3** characters per season to complete the trial get it. When the third is taken, the trial room seals with a server announcement **(Claude's call, Jay can veto the number)**.
- Effect: equip a second sword in the shield hand. Off-hand adds an extra hit per attack at 50% damage, unlocks 3 exclusive dual-blade sword skills, and you lose the shield's defense. Balanced so it is a distinct style, not a must-have.
- If a Twin Fang holder dies on Death Game or the season ends, the slot reopens next season (not mid-season) **(Claude's call)**.

## 6. PvP and community health (top priority)

Goal: real PvP that creates stories, with strong protection against strong guilds farming weaker players.

### 6.1 Cursors (SAO style, mapped onto Tibia skulls)
Our client colors the name and a cursor above each player:

| Cursor | Tibia skull | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | none | clean player |
| **Orange** | white / yellow / orange skull | attacked someone recently or committed a recent unjust kill; attacking them is safe |
| **Red** | red skull | murderer |
| **Dark red with black outline** | black skull | serial murderer **(Claude's call)** |

### 6.2 Safe zones
Towns only (Jay's rule): town streets, temple, depot, bank, shops, houses. No attacks of any kind inside.

### 6.3 Zone tiers (outside towns) — from Jay's own input in the old pack
| Tier | Where | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Guarded** | roads and fields near towns, Floor 1 starter fields | PvP possible, but an unjust kill here counts as 3 unjust kills (straight to red) and town guards hunt the killer for 30 min |
| **Open** | most hunting grounds, labyrinths, the Underkeep's first wings | normal skull rules |
| **Contested** | each floor's apex spawn | kills don't count as unjust and give no skull; killers earn Blood Marks (spent on supplies/cosmetics). Victims drop more items. Newbie protection still applies |
| **Deadzone** | apex inner layers, the Underkeep's deepest wings | Contested rules + bigger losses + the best XP/loot on the floor. Warning sign and a "Are you sure?" on first entry |

The current zone is shown in the corner of the client (icon + color).

### 6.4 Protections against farming weaker players **(Claude's call on all numbers, Jay can veto any of them)**

1. **Newbie protection:** below **level 50** you can't attack players and players can't attack you, everywhere except inside boss rooms **and you can't enter Contested/Deadzone zones** (the border blocks you). A new character also has 2 hours of play time protection regardless of level.
2. **Level-range protection:** outside Contested/Deadzone and boss rooms, you can't start a fight with a player below **70% of your level** (a level 200 can't attack a level 139). They can still defend themselves, and once they hit you, you can hit back. Skulled players (orange/red/black) are never protected by this.
3. **Outnumbered shield:** if 3 or more different players have hit you in the last 10 seconds, you take 10% less player damage per attacker beyond 2 (max 40% less). Fights stay winnable for small teams with good play (Jay's "fair PvP" memory) and masses of 4–10 lose their steamroll.
4. **Guild-farming shield:** if members of the same guild kill you twice within 6 hours outside a war, you get a 30-minute **Sanctuary**: that guild can't attack you and you can't attack them. Repeated triggers are logged for staff review.
5. **Bounties on red players:**
   - Every red skull automatically gets a system bounty paid from the killer's own fines.
   - Anyone can add gold to a bounty at the Bounty Board in any town.
   - Collected by killing the red player. Never pays out to the same IP, same guild, same party, or anyone who partied with the target in the last 24 hours (stops friends cashing in). *(Idea from Cyntara's WANTED system, which also refuses same-IP payouts.)*
6. **Consent-based guild wars:** a war only exists if both guild leaders sign it at the Guild Registrar (fee from both, sink). Terms set at signing: length (1–14 days), optional kill limit, optional surrender fee. War kills never cause skulls. Neither side can force a war; a guild can't re-declare on the same guild for 7 days after it ends.
7. **Harsh red-skull penalties:**

| Skull | You get it for | Lasts | Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orange (cursor) | 1–2 unjust kills in 24 h | 30 min per kill, max 2 h | killing you is justified |
| **Red** | 3 unjust kills in 24 h, or 5 in 7 days, or 10 in 30 days | 7 days after the last unjust kill | lose your whole backpack **and** equipment on death; death penalty ×2; can't enter town gates (guards attack); NPC trade only at the Outlaw Camp; can't enter boss rooms; automatic bounty |
| **Black** | 6 unjust kills in 24 h, or 10 in 7 days | 14 days after the last unjust kill | red penalties + can't use teleport gates or party-share XP; guards attack on sight |

8. **Bully multiplier:** an unjust kill on a player 30+ levels below you counts **double** toward skulls (except in Contested/Deadzone).

### 6.5 New-player welcome
- Guild recruitment board, a "looking for guild" flag and LFG chat channel (Jay: finding a clan was the hardest part).
- Mentor mode for parties lets high-level guildmates bring new players along (from the old pack).

### 6.6 Death Game characters and player killers
**Rule: a Death Game character is only deleted by PvE.** **(Claude's call on mechanism, Jay can veto)**
- If **any other player** (not a party member, guildmate, or same-IP character) damaged the Death Game character in the 60 seconds before death, the death counts as a **PvP death**: the character is **not deleted**. It takes the Hardcore death penalty instead and respawns in the temple.
- Party members, guildmates and same-IP characters don't count, so a friend can't "tag" you with 1 damage to make you immortal.
- Death Game characters are also covered by newbie and level-range protection like everyone, and killing an unskulled Death Game character counts as **two** unjust kills.
- Every saved death is logged; staff can review patterns.

## 8. Exploration

### 8.1 Secrets
Hidden shops, dungeons, quests, bosses, fishing spots, NPCs, chests and Easter eggs. Target: **12 secrets per floor** at launch (120 total) **(Claude's call)**, with a mix of practical (dungeon, shop, quest reward) and flashy/silly (Jay: he'd sell the flashy ones and keep the useful ones).

**How it works (Jay's design):**
1. Each secret has a trigger point (a tile, a lever, an NPC phrase, a fishing catch, a timed event).
2. When a player touches or passes the trigger, the discovery is recorded as **theirs** (with time and floor). Message: *"You discovered <secret>. The Info Broker would pay for this."*
3. They can **sell** it to the Info Broker NPC (**Vesper**, original name) or **keep it private**.
4. **Selling** pays gold by tier (small / medium / large × floor) and makes the secret **public**: server-wide announcement (*"<player> has sold word of <secret> on Floor 4!"*) and it appears on the broker's list with directions. The seller gets an "Informant" credit on their profile.
5. **Keeping it private never locks anyone out.** Anyone else can find it the normal way. It's simply their secret until someone sells it.
6. Once public, nobody else can sell it. Players who found it earlier keep their discovery record (it still counts for explorer titles).

**Claude's calls:** sale payout = floor base × tier (small ×1, medium ×2, large ×4). Account-wide **explorer titles** carry across seasons (10, 50, 100 discoveries; "Cartographer" for finding every secret on a floor). A secret's interior is never required for the main path (floors, labyrinths, story quests).

### 8.2 Fishing
- Tibia's fishing skill, expanded **(Claude's call on all details)**:
  - Every floor has its own fish list; some fish only bite at night, in rain, during events, or with special bait.
  - Catches: food fish (cook into small regen buffs, no combat stats), gold-value fish, rare cosmetics, treasure chests, **message bottles that start secrets**, and one **legendary fish per floor**.
  - Fishing collection log, account-wide, with titles ("Angler", "Master Angler", "Tower Angler"), carried across seasons.
  - Weekly Fishing Derby (biggest catch in 1 hour wins a trophy and gold).
  - Hidden fishing spots are secrets.
  - Anti-bot: a fish that "struggles" needs one extra click within a few seconds; unattended fishing catches only junk.

### 8.3 Quests
Every floor has story quests and side quests. Every quest has a quest-log entry that names the next step. No required puzzle longer than about 15 minutes once understood. Hints exist in game (Jay hated unexplained hour-long quests). Each floor's story quests unlock a **perk choice** (pick 1 of 2), earned only, never sold *(from the old pack)*.

## 12. Bots and automation

Jay's rules (2026-11-08): *"Cave bot will be built in, but when it's enabled xp gain will be significantly reduced. Training manually will be much more beneficial. But it can be an option for when they afk or what not. The risk of course is being pked. Auto targeting is also going to need to be built in."*

**Background research:** Cyntara's written rules ban macros and outside programs, but its own client ships helper tools (a walker, a looter, experimental vBot support) and some rewards stay hidden from players who used the auto-walker recently. Spirebound goes further in the same direction: the helpers, cavebot included, are **built into our client**, and the game makes manual play clearly better.

**Built into the Spirebound client (allowed):**
- **Cavebot**: walks a route you record, attacks what it meets, loots, and uses supplies. Usable when you're away from the keyboard.
- **Auto-targeting**: picks the next monster for you.
- Auto-heal (spell or potion at the HP/mana % you set), auto-eat, ring/amulet refill, loot helper, hotkeys.

**The cost of the cavebot (Claude's call on numbers, Jay can veto):**
- While the cavebot is on, you get **30% of normal XP** and **50% of normal loot**. Manual hunting is more than 3× better.
- The reduction stays for **10 minutes after you switch it off**, so nobody flicks it off just for a boss or a rare spawn.
- Cavebot characters show a small **gear icon** next to their name, so everyone can see who's botting. Being PKed is the risk you take (Jay); all normal PvP rules and protections still apply.
- Stamina still drains as usual.
- Secrets, rare chests and the Twin Fang trial never trigger while the cavebot is on, or within 10 minutes of using it.
- Boss rooms (field and floor bosses) refuse to let a cavebot character in.
- **Auto-targeting on its own (no cavebot) costs nothing.** It's a convenience for players at the keyboard **(Claude's call)**.

**Not allowed:**
- Any third-party bot or modified client. Our client is the only allowed client.
- Auto-fishing (fishing has its own anti-AFK "struggle" click) **(Claude's call)**.
- Cheating the cavebot penalty (e.g. a hacked client that hides the cavebot) = immediate ban.

**Enforcement:**
- The client tells the server when the cavebot is on, and the server applies the XP/loot cut. The server also logs patterns that look like an unmarked bot (identical routes for 20+ minutes, 24-hour sessions with steady XP, impossibly fast reactions) for staff review. No automatic bans.
- Punishment for third-party bots or a hidden cavebot: first offense 7-day ban and removal of gains; second offense permanent.
- Up to 2 clients per computer at once **(Claude's call)**.
- Rules are published on the website and in game.
