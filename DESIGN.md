## 1. What Spirebound is

An Open Tibia server shaped like a floating tower. Everyone starts on Floor 1. Each floor ends in a labyrinth with a boss room. The first time anyone kills a floor boss, the next floor opens for the whole server with a server-wide announcement. Players pick a difficulty when they create a character, all the way up to **Death Game**, where dying deletes the character.

It plays like Tibia (hunting, loot, skulls, houses, guilds, depots, parties, the four familiar vocations, gold/platinum/crystal coins) with SAO-style touches: a floor indicator, sword skills on a hotbar, green/orange/red player cursors, an Info Broker who buys secrets, and a hidden dungeon under Floor 1 that grows as the tower opens.

### Pillars (what every decision is checked against)
1. **The tower is shared.** Floors open for everyone. The server has one story, written by its players.
2. **PvP with teeth, not bullying.** Fights are real, but the game actively protects weaker and newer players. Toxicity is why Jay has quit servers, so this is the top priority.
3. **Exploration pays.** Secrets are everywhere and worth money, bragging rights, or both.
4. **Houses do things.** Houses are functional: portals, private boss rooms, gambling halls, gardens.
5. **Never pay-to-win.** The best gear and spells come only from playing. (Jay, 2026-11-23: "the shop should have some gear that gets outclassed later": shop starter gear is allowed, but gear earned by playing always beats it past the early levels.)
6. **Plain to learn.** Every quest says what to do next. No hour-long puzzles you can't figure out (Jay's own pain point).

### Naming rule
Original names inspired by SAO. No SAO character, place, item, or skill names anywhere a player can see. `tools/banned_names.txt` + `tools/check_names.py` enforce this on every build. Same for CipSoft monster/NPC/city names.

## 3. The tower

### 3.1 Floors
- 10 floors at launch. More added in updates.
- Each floor: a town (Floor 1 has two), hunting grounds, one "apex" spawn (see 3.5), a **field boss** guarding the only pass, a **labyrinth** behind the pass, and the **floor boss** room at the top of the labyrinth.
- Floor 1 is open at launch. Every other floor is sealed until its unlock.

| Floor | Name | Theme | Level band (hunting target) | Floor boss | **(Claude's call)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hearthgate Plains | meadows, ruins, windmills, river | 1–35 | Axe-Lord of the Stair | names and bands |
| 2 | Highrest Highlands | cliffs, herds, bone mesas | 35–60 | Crowned Aurochs | |
| 3 | Rootwell Fogwood | fog forest, giant roots | 60–90 | Sleeping Trunk (field boss: Mossback Warden) | |
| 4 | Lakehold | lake castle, islands, boats | 90–120 | Knight of the Closed Helm (field boss: Lake Knight) | |
| 5 | Ringhold | arena city, colosseum ruins | 120–160 | Empty Colossus (field boss: Arena Warden) | |
| 6 | Emberreach | volcanic badlands, forges | 160–200 | Cinder Matriarch | |
| 7 | Glasswater Marsh | swamp, crystal reeds | 200–250 | The Many-Mouthed | |
| 8 | Frostspire | snow peaks, ice caves | 250–300 | Rimebound Jarl | |
| 9 | The Hollow Bazaar | ruined merchant city, catacombs | 300–350 | The Ledger King | |
| 10 | The Cobalt Labyrinth | blue-lit stone maze | 350–400+ | **Azhrakkar, the Cerulean Ruin** (see 9.3) | |

Level bands are hunting targets, not doors. No level cap. Floors 2–5 use the October pack's bosses and monsters; every floor's roster lives in `maps/floors.py` **(Claude's call, 2026-11-08)**.

### 3.2 How a floor unlocks
1. The **field boss** at the pass must die once (anyone, any time). After that the labyrinth door is open forever for everyone.
2. The **floor boss** room is at the end of the labyrinth. Up to 20 players inside. PvP is on inside boss rooms.
3. **First kill of a floor boss:**
   - opens the stair and the teleport gate to the next floor for everyone;
   - plays a server-wide gold announcement + bell: *"Floor 3 has been cleared by <names>! The way to Floor 4 is open."*;
   - opens the next wing of the Floor 1 hidden dungeon (3.4);
   - starts the house head-start window for the new floor's town (7.4);
   - carves the credited names on the Monument in Hearthgate (and later on the website).
4. **Credit:** anyone who damaged the boss, or healed someone who did, in the last 90 seconds. Top 20 by contribution if more qualify. Credited players get a floor title ("Gatebreaker", "Hornbreaker", …), a cosmetic weapon glow, and the first-kill loot. The **last hit** gets the title "Last Blow (Floor N)" and a bound Last Blow Shard (an upgrade material, not a weapon or stats). *(From the old pack.)*
5. **Enrage scales with headcount** so a stuck server can still be unstuck by a small group: enrage timer = 8 min × 8 ÷ players (max 60 min). Solo = 60 min, 4 players = 16 min, 8+ = 8 min. *(From the old pack.)*
6. **Repeat kills:** after the first kill the boss becomes repeatable from a portal beside the stair for parties of 1–5, once per character per 20 hours **(Claude's call)**.

### 3.3 Towns and safe zones
- Hearthgate (Floor 1) is our echo of SAO's starting city: same feeling (big temple plaza, where everyone begins and returns), original name and layout.
- **Safe zones are in towns only** (Jay's rule). Inside towns, depots and houses nobody can attack anyone. Everywhere else PvP is possible, with the protections in section 6.

### 3.4 The Floor 1 hidden dungeon: the Underkeep
- A dark stone dungeon under Hearthgate. Its entrance is itself a secret (drain grate by the river).
- One map that grows. A new wing opens each time **two** floors are cleared **(Claude's call)**: Wing 1 at launch (once found), Wing 2 when Floor 2 opens, Wing 3 at Floor 4, Wing 4 at Floor 6, Wing 5 at Floor 8, Wing 6 at Floor 10. Later seasons add more wings.
- Each wing is tuned to the floor that opened it, not to the player. A level 10 in Wing 5 should leave fast.
- Every wing hides at least one more secret (shop, boss, chest room, fishing pool).
- Sealed doors read: *"Sealed until the tower reaches Floor N."*
- **Wing 1 as a real dungeon (Jay, 2026-11-26, Q89: "yes. being honest i dont know how many people im going to get to help me play test. i may end up just being my self or maybe myself and one other person."):** corridors two or three tiles wide, guard rooms, cells, a collapsed and a flooded part, at least one loop and dead ends with rewards, one secret inside, about 50 monsters of three kinds and a mini-boss. Because the first playtests may be Jay alone or with one other person, everything on Floor 1 must be testable solo or as a pair (the Pilot party tools stand in for a party).

### 3.4b Small game in the open country (Jay, 2026-11-26, Q94: "fill in the empty land with small game monsters to help it feel less empty")
- The empty lawns between Floor 1's grounds hold harmless or nearly harmless small creatures in loose scatter, so the land feels alive. **(Claude's call, Jay can veto:)** original names, their own habits, tiny experience and loot so they never become the best farm, never on roads or near a ground's gate.

### 3.5 Apex spawns (from Jay's favorite hunt)
One layered, over-tuned spawn per floor that stays worth hunting long past its floor: an easy outer layer, a packed inner room, a semi-hidden rope spot to a big deeper spawn, and a hidden side dungeon with easier, denser monsters. Apex spawns sit in **Contested** PvP zones (6.3), so they create the fights Jay remembers.

### 3.6 Something always happening
Every 3 hours a world event fires on an open floor: Frenzy (apex spawn +50% respawn, +25% loot), Incursion (elite packs), Fishing Frenzy (rare-fish rate up), Wandering Champion (roaming mini-boss). Announced 10 minutes ahead. *(From the old pack, fishing event added.)*

**Floor Beacons (Jay, 2026-11-25: "r1 do it", from Grok's market research R1).** Players choose where the boost goes, instead of the server picking at random.
- Each open floor's town has a **Beacon of 4 fire bowls** (Hearthgate: east of the Monument; other towns: by the temple door). Players use a bowl with a **Beacon Ember** in their backpack (or use the Ember on it) to light it for **1 hour**.
- **Embers** (one kind, works on any floor's Beacon): field bosses always drop 2, hidden mini-bosses 1, elites 8% of the time, apex monsters 1.5%. (There are no taskmaster bosses yet; if they come, they drop Embers too.) *(Claude's call, Jay can veto.)*
- Each lit bowl gives everyone hunting on that floor **+5% experience and an extra 5% loot roll**. With **all 4 lit**, the floor's apex spawn refills fast: 3 extra apex monsters at its heart, each back 90 seconds after it dies. No damage bonus, so Contested PvP zones stay fair. `!beacon` shows the fires; the floor label shows "Beacon 2/4 (+10%, 45 min)".
- Every lighting is **announced server-wide with the names of who lit it**; the floor HUD shows how many slots are lit and for how long.
- Embers can be traded, so they are worth money. **One guild can hold at most 2 slots on a floor at a time** (stops a rich guild from keeping a floor lit for itself). *(Numbers: Claude's call, Jay can veto.)*
- The boosted creature of the day (roadmap J7) stays for now; it's a daily reason to try a different ground. *(Claude's call, Jay can veto.)*

### 3.7 Waycrystals (Jay's idea, 2026-11-26, Q94: "i would lean towards fill in the empty land with small game monsters to help it feel less empty. i had an idea to incorporate 'teleport crystals' to help players save time, to create a unique system, and to provide another area to burn player income. the teleport crystal can be set to instantly teleport you to town, or it can open a menu and teleport you to a training zone on that floor specifically. teleport crystals should come in 10 different colors for each floor and labeled accordingly.")
- Jay's own: ten crystals, one per floor, each its own colour and labelled with its floor; using one takes you to that floor's town, or opens a menu of that floor's hunting grounds. A time-saver and a gold sink.
- **(Claude's call, Jay can veto; Q95):** called Waycrystals ("Teleport Crystal" is SAO's item name). Right-click: a menu of the floor's town and the grounds whose level band you have reached (you arrive at the gate). Used up on arrival. Three seconds standing still; moving or taking damage cancels it; refused in a fight (any hit given or taken in the last few seconds), in boss rooms, arenas, the tutorial and party caves, and for floors not yet open. Sold by each floor's Runewright (never the donation shop) at about 4 minutes of that floor's hunting income (Floor 1: 150 gold); it replaces the old temple scroll. Prices come from `tools/balance.py`, which counts them as a sink.

## 4. Seasons

Jay's brief: 20–30 floors per season with seasonal resets; account-wide rewards carry across seasons. Recommend 100 permanent floors instead only if analysis says seasons won't hold a player base.

### Analysis and recommendation: **keep seasons.**
- Seasonal resets are a proven OT model. Cyntara (the server Jay remembers best) runs on seasons and is on its 19th version; its launch week is what Jay remembers most fondly. Every reset is a new "launch week" with a fresh race, which is the strongest population spike an OT server gets.
- Seasons directly fix the toxicity problem Jay cares about most: on permanent servers the level gap between veterans and newcomers grows forever, and that gap is what lets top guilds farm new players. A reset closes the gap every few months.
- 100 permanent floors would need roughly 4–10× the content before the server feels "complete", and the world-first race would happen only once per floor, ever.
- **Jay confirmed seasons on 2026-11-08** ("Seasons are a go").

### Season rules **(Claude's call, Jay can veto)**
- Season length: about **5 months**. Season 1 launches with 10 floors; content updates add floors through the season, target **25 floors** by season end (inside Jay's 20–30 range).
- Floors are built once and **remixed** in later seasons (new bosses mechanics, shuffled secrets, new Underkeep wings), so content keeps paying off.
- **Resets:** characters, levels, items, gold, houses, guilds' houses, and secret discoveries.
- **Carries over (account-wide):** titles, cosmetic outfits and mounts, Death Game titles, fishing collection log, explorer titles, donation-shop purchases (cosmetics), Hall of Fame entries (first clears, Death Game leaderboard).
- Old-season characters are frozen in a read-only "Hall of Seasons" on the website (name, level, achievements).
- Final two weeks of a season: "Last Stand" events (double XP weekends, the season's final boss rushes), announced well ahead.

### Spire Chronicle: a season-long journey (Jay, 2026-11-25: "love the idea", from Grok's research R3)
- A **5-chapter goal track per season, shared across your whole account** (all your characters count). Chapters 1–2 teach the game (a guide for new players); chapters 3–5 ask for floor clears, Underkeep wings and secrets.
- Finishing it gives a **permanent Chronicle seal and title** that upgrades each season, a line on the Monument and a house trophy. **No experience or power carries over** into the next season: cosmetics and bragging rights only (seasons stay a fair race).
- It sits above the Season Path (Season Path = daily and weekly goals; Chronicle = the season-long story), and finished Chronicles show in the Hall of Seasons. *(Details: Claude's call, Jay can veto.)*
- **The five chapters** *(Claude's call, Jay can veto)*: 1 *Arrival*: level 8, choose a path, 100 monsters, a quest. 2 *The Climber's Trade*: level 20, a hunting task, 5 fish, a secret. 3 *Through the Pass*: a field boss, a floor boss, stand on Floor 2, light a Beacon fire. 4 *Under the Keep*: find the Underkeep, 5 secrets, a hidden beast, level 50. 5 *The Season's Climb*: stand on Floor 4, 5 floor bosses, 12 secrets, level 100.
- Every character on the account adds to the same counts (levels and floors: the best any of them reached). Botting doesn't count. The seal's title grows with seasons finished: Chronicler, Seasoned Chronicler, Elder Chronicler, Grand Chronicler. The house trophy is a Chronicle Cup. Journal tab "Chronicle", or `!chronicle`.

## 5. Characters

### 5.1 Creating a character
Account is created on the website (or, while testing locally, by a script). A new character wakes in the **Arrival Yard** in Hearthgate with no vocation. A short guided path (under 10 minutes) teaches movement, combat, looting and the skill bar, and ends at the Registrar NPC, where the player picks:
1. **Vocation** (5.2)
2. **Difficulty mode** (5.4)
(Choosing in-game rather than on a website form works without a website and makes the choice feel like a moment. **Claude's call, Jay can veto.**)

### 5.2 Vocations (four, familiar to Tibia players)

| Spirebound name | Tibia equivalent | Weapon families (each has its own sword skills) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vanguard** | Knight | Sword, Axe, Club (+ shield) | Tank, front line |
| **Ranger** | Paladin | Bow, Crossbow, Spear (throwing) | Ranged damage, some healing |
| **Arcanist** | Sorcerer | Wand ("spellblade" arts) | Area damage, big flashy spells |
| **Mystic** | Druid | Rod ("spellblade" arts) | Healer, support, nature damage |

Names are **(Claude's call, Jay can veto)**. Vocations keep Tibia's familiar feel: hit points, mana, capacity, skills (sword/axe/club/distance/shielding/magic level/fishing), and promotion at level 20 (Vanguard → Blade Warden, Ranger → Far Warden, Arcanist → High Arcanist, Mystic → Elder Mystic) for the familiar regeneration bump **(Claude's call)**.

Every vocation unlocks about 20 spells, including utility and support spells (Jay), listed in 5.3b. Melee and ranged attack power comes mostly from **sword skills**; Arcanists and Mystics also get big attack spells.

### 5.3 Sword skills
Every vocation gets sword skills: combo-style abilities with cooldowns, tied to the weapon type in hand. Change weapon type, change skill list.
- **Hotbar:** a dedicated sword-skill bar in our client (up to 6 slots), separate from normal hotkeys. Skills glow when ready and show their cooldown.
- **Unlocks:** each weapon family has 6 skills: 3 from the start, then one each at levels 60, 100 and 150 **(Claude's call; weapon proficiency or quests can replace the level gates later)**.
- **Combos:** every skill is tagged *Opener*, *Link* or *Finisher*. Opener → Link → Finisher within 4 seconds of each other is a **combo**: the Finisher deals +30% and the combo counter on the bar lights up. Skills still work alone; combos are a skill reward, not a requirement **(Claude's call)**.
- **Cooldowns** are enforced by the server (a bot gains nothing from fast clicking). Each skill has its own cooldown plus a short shared 1-second "skill" cooldown.
- **Look:** sword skills must be "captivating to watch" (Jay). Each gets a distinct effect; big ones get bigger art budgets in `ART_REQUESTS.md`.
- First skill set (examples; full list lives in the skill data file, `server/.../sword_skills.lua`):
  - Sword: *Arc Slash* (Opener, front 3 tiles), *Riposte Line* (Link, line of 3), *Fourfold Rend* (Finisher, 4 rapid hits on one target)
  - Axe: *Split Earth* (Opener, area around you), *Hewing Turn* (Link), *Executioner's Drop* (Finisher, +damage vs targets under 30%)
  - Club: *Bell Ringer* (Opener, 1 s stun on monsters), *Crushing Arc* (Link), *Tremor Blow* (Finisher, area)
  - Bow: *Quickdraw* (Opener), *Pinning Shot* (Link, slow), *Arrow Storm* (Finisher, area)
  - Crossbow: *Bolt Lance* (Opener, line), *Ratchet Shot* (Link), *Siege Bolt* (Finisher)
  - Spear: *Skewer* (Opener), *Javelin Arc* (Link), *Comet Spear* (Finisher)
  - Wand (Arcanist): *Spark Edge* (Opener), *Flame Script* (Link, area), *Starfall Rite* (Finisher, big area)
  - Rod (Mystic): *Thorn Edge* (Opener), *Frost Script* (Link, slow), *Verdant Burst* (Finisher, damage + small party heal)

### 5.3b Spells (Jay, 2026-11-08: "Each voc should have a decent amount of spells they can unlock. Make sure there are utility spells and support spells as well.")

Sword skills are the combo attacks. **Spells** are everything else: healing, support, utility, crowd control, runes, and (for the two mage vocations) the big magic. **The full design is `docs/design/spells.md`** (overhaul of 2026-11-13, Jay's brief: 15–25 original spells per vocation, a different unlock rhythm for each, roles kept, Tibia's solo curve with the Vanguard hardest and the Ranger easiest, a distinct look per vocation). Names, levels and numbers are **(Claude's call, Jay can veto any of them)**; exact numbers live in `server/data-spirebound/lib/spirebound/spells.lua`.

**How spells unlock:**
- Spells **unlock automatically when you reach their level** (Jay, 2026-11-08: "spells should be learned on level unlock, not gold"). No trainer, no cost. A message tells you what you just learned; `!spells` lists them.
- **2 per vocation are "Lost Arts"**: earned only by finishing every story quest on Floor 5 (level 120+) and Floor 8 (level 250+), never sold **(Claude's call)**.
- **Ammunition can't be conjured** (Jay). Arrows, bolts and spears are bought from NPCs, alongside potions; together they are the main everyday gold sink. Rune spells use up a blank rune.
- Incantations are our own words (not Tibia's), short and easy to type. Everything can also sit on a hotkey.

**What each vocation gets** (6 shared utilities, each learned at that vocation's own level, plus its own spells):

| Vocation | Own spells | Rhythm | Highlights |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vanguard | 28 (34 in all) | slow burn, defense first, late power | Heavy Strike 8, Shield Bash 12, Iron Reach 22, Iron Hide 30, Whirlwind 33 (deliberately weak), Standard of Defiance 55, Skyrend 90, Undying Oath 200, Aegis Ascendant 230, Mountainbreaker 300 |
| Ranger | 30 (36) | steady, kiting tools early | Piercing Shot 8, Scatter Shot 16, Recoil Shot 18, Snare 20, Ricochet 35, Sunburst Volley 45, Falling Star 72, Dawnpiercer 80, Dawnstrider 230, Constellation 300 |
| Arcanist | 29 (35) | front-loaded glass cannon | Ember Strike 8, Forked Arc 16, Fireburst Wave 18, Gravity Well 42, Stormcall 55, Skybrand 70, Inferno Core 80, Cinder Lance 100, Stormheart 230, Worldfire 300 |
| Mystic | 31 (37) | heals first, party tools mid-game | Frost Strike 8, Mend Other 16, Bloomburst 30, Mass Mend 36, Thornfield 55, Linked Mend 60, Lifeseed 75, Verdant Renewal 100, Wildheart 230, Worldroot 300 |

Shared utilities: Kindle (light), Wayfinder (find a player), Cleanse, Skystep (climb a level), Hearth Ration (food), Quickstep (haste).

Second pass (2026-11-17, Jay's brief to "spice up the spells"): area spells play out over time, telegraphed strikes, chains, knock-backs, roots, ground zones, forms at 230, capstones at 300, spells that look bigger as you level, **Elemental Clash** (two different elements in a row on one monster: +25% and a named burst), and 18 effects of our own. ### 5.4 Difficulty modes
Chosen at creation. You can move **down** a difficulty at the Registrar (Death Game → Hardcore → Normal), **never up**.

| | Normal | Hardcore | Death Game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Death | Standard Tibia death penalty | Heavier penalty (below) | **Character is deleted** (retired to the Memorial) |
| XP bonus | — | +25% | +50% |
| Loot bonus | — | +15% | +30% |
| Cursor tag | none | small flame icon | skull-and-blade icon |
| Exclusive | — | Hardcore title | Death Game title, exclusive outfit, own leaderboard |

Bonus percentages are **(Claude's call, Jay can veto)**.

**Normal = standard Tibia:** you lose a share of your experience and skill progress on death, reduced by blessings bought from the temple priest; with no blessings and no amulet you can drop items. Red/black skulls lose more (6.4).

**Hardcore (Claude's call):** experience and skill loss ×1.5. Blessings can only reduce the penalty by half (not fully). Item drop chance on death doubled. No amulet that prevents item loss. *How it works (engine limits, Claude's call):* the extra skill loss is a "skill debt" — half of what the death took must be re-trained before new progress counts (the engine can't remove skill progress from a script). Items get a second 10% roll per equipped item, which works out to exactly "doubled, blessings only remove half". An amulet of loss shatters on a Hardcore death and protects nothing.

**Death Game (Claude's call on details):**
- Death by monsters, bosses, traps or environment deletes the character. In practice: the character is **retired**, locked forever, and placed on the Memorial wall in Hearthgate and the website with level, floor reached and cause of death. The account keeps every title and cosmetic already earned.
- Exclusive rewards: title "Deathbound" on reaching level 50, the exclusive Death Game outfit at level 100 (outfit addons at 200/300), leaderboard by level and floors cleared. All are account-wide and carry across seasons.
- **Moving down:** a Death Game character can step down to Hardcore or Normal any time in town. It keeps cosmetics already earned, loses its leaderboard spot (listed as "stepped down at level X"), and can never return to Death Game.
- **Player killers can never delete a Death Game character.** See 6.6.

- Medium rates with stages (faster early, slower later), **no level cap**. Starting stages **(Claude's call, tuned after playtests)**. Levels 1-50 were changed by Jay's answer to Q30 (2026-11-12): Floor 1 should last about 11-15 hours (FLOOR1_WILDS_PLAN.md Part 3). Levels 51+ get the same check when Floor 2 is planned (R10):

|---|---|
| 1–8 | 10× |
| 9–20 | 4× |
| 21–50 | 3× |
| 51–100 | 15× |
| 101–150 | 10× |
| 151–200 | 7× |
| 201–300 | 5× |
| 301–400 | 3× |

- Skill rate 10×, magic level 5×, loot rate 2×, fishing 5× **(Claude's call)**.
- **Currency:** Tibia's gold, platinum and crystal coins (100 gold = 1 platinum, 100 platinum = 1 crystal). Bank in every town.
- **Stamina:** standard Tibia stamina (full XP for the first hours of a day's play, reduced after) **(Claude's call, it also limits botting; Cyntara uses a similar system)**.

### 5.6 Dual wielding: the rare unique skill "Twin Fang"
- Earned in-game only. Never sold.
- Hidden trial on Floor 8 (itself a secret). To enter: Vanguard, sword skill 80+, and you must have finished a sword-skill combo 1,000 times **(Claude's call)**.
- **Rare:** only the first **3** characters per season to complete the trial get it. When the third is taken, the trial room seals with a server announcement **(Claude's call, Jay can veto the number)**.
- Effect: fight with a sword and an **empty shield hand**: every melee hit is followed by an off-hand strike at 50% damage, and 3 exclusive sword skills unlock (Twin Fang Rush, Crossed Fangs, Fang Storm). You give up the shield's defense. Balanced so it is a distinct style, not a must-have. (The engine can't hold two weapons at once, so the second blade is the off-hand strike. **Claude's call, Jay can veto.**)
- The trial: a stone circle hidden in Floor 8's apex cave. Step on it with the requirements met and the Twin Fang Warden rises; defeat it to claim the skill.
- If a Twin Fang holder dies on Death Game or the season ends, the slot reopens next season (not mid-season) **(Claude's call)**.

## 6. PvP and community health (top priority)

Goal: real PvP that creates stories, with strong protection against strong guilds farming weaker players.

### 6.1 Cursors (SAO style, mapped onto Tibia skulls)
Our client colors the name and a cursor above each player:

| Cursor | Tibia skull | Meaning |

### 6.4 Protections against farming weaker players **(Claude's call on all numbers, Jay can veto any of them)**

1. **Newbie protection:** below **level 50** you can't attack players and players can't attack you, everywhere except inside boss rooms, Contested and Deadzone ground included: you may hunt there, but only players of 50 and above fight each other on it *(Claude's call, 2026-11-25: the old border kept everyone under 50 out, and Floors 1-4's apex grounds are hunted below 50)*. A new character also has 2 hours of play time protection regardless of level.
2. **Level-range protection:** outside Contested/Deadzone and boss rooms, you can't start a fight with a player below **70% of your level** (a level 200 can't attack a level 139). They can still defend themselves, and once they hit you, you can hit back. Skulled players (orange/red/black) are never protected by this.
3. **Outnumbered shield:** if 3 or more different players have hit you in the last 10 seconds, you take 10% less player damage per attacker beyond 2 (max 40% less). Fights stay winnable for small teams with good play (Jay's "fair PvP" memory) and masses of 4–10 lose their steamroll.
4. **Guild-farming shield:** if members of the same guild kill you twice within 6 hours outside a war, you get a 30-minute **Sanctuary**: that guild can't attack you and you can't attack them. Repeated triggers are logged for staff review.
5. **Bounties on red players:**
   - Every red skull automatically gets a system bounty paid from the killer's own fines.
   - Anyone can add gold to a bounty at the Bounty Board in any town.
   - Collected by killing the red player. Never pays out to the same IP, same guild, same party, or anyone who partied with the target in the last 24 hours (stops friends cashing in). *(Idea from Cyntara's WANTED system, which also refuses same-IP payouts.)*
6. **Consent-based guild wars:** a war only exists if both guild leaders sign it at the Guild Registrar (fee from both, sink). Terms set at signing: length (1–14 days), optional kill limit, optional surrender fee. War kills never cause skulls. Neither side can force a war; a guild can't re-declare on the same guild for 7 days after it ends.
7. **Harsh red-skull penalties:**

| Skull | You get it for | Lasts | Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orange (cursor) | 1–2 unjust kills in 24 h | 30 min per kill, max 2 h | killing you is justified |
| **Red** | 3 unjust kills in 24 h, or 5 in 7 days, or 10 in 30 days | 7 days after the last unjust kill | lose your whole backpack **and** equipment on death; death penalty ×2; can't enter town gates (guards attack); NPC trade only at the Outlaw Camp; can't enter boss rooms; automatic bounty |
| **Black** | 6 unjust kills in 24 h, or 10 in 7 days | 14 days after the last unjust kill | red penalties + can't use teleport gates or party-share XP; guards attack on sight |

8. **Bully multiplier:** an unjust kill on a player 30+ levels below you counts **double** toward skulls (except in Contested/Deadzone).

**Fair credit (roadmap R7, 2026-11-13, Claude's call on the numbers):** everyone who did at least 10% of the victim's health in the last minute shares a kill: each of them can earn the unjust kill, each of their guilds counts toward Sanctuary, and a bounty is split among them by damage (never to the target's party, guild, same-IP or anyone who partied with the target in the last 24 hours). The outnumbered shield counts only outside attackers who did at least 5% in the last 10 seconds. Healing a white, red or black skulled player removes your own newbie and level-range protection for 5 minutes. Murderers who die wake in the **Outlaw Camp** in the far west, not in a temple.

### 6.6 Death Game characters and player killers
**Rule: a Death Game character is only deleted by PvE.** **(Claude's call on mechanism, Jay can veto)**
- If **any other player** (not a party member, guildmate, or same-IP character) did **real damage** (at least 15% of max health) to the Death Game character in the 60 seconds before death, the death counts as a **PvP death**: the character is **not deleted**. It takes the Hardcore death penalty instead and respawns in the temple. (Roadmap R5, 2026-11-13.)
- A player's killing blow never deletes the character. A friend's killing blow only counts if that friend really fought (15%), so a friend can't "save" you by landing the last hit on a character the monsters were killing.
- **Dragged monsters:** if the monster that killed you hit another outside player in the last 30 seconds, it was dragged in: not PvE, not deleted.
- Party members, guildmates and same-IP characters don't count, so a friend can't "tag" you with 1 damage to make you immortal.
- **Retirement** frees the character's house, passes guild leadership to the next-highest member (or closes the guild), removes market offers, frees the character slot, and saves at once.
- **Unfair deaths:** a server crash or server lag is put right by a GM with `/restore <name>`; a player's own internet dropping is not (Q14, assumed).
- **Stepping down** costs the extra experience the harder mode's bonus gave (Q14, assumed). **Deathbound** is earned only by reaching level 50 alive on the Death Game.
- Death Game characters are also covered by newbie and level-range protection like everyone, and killing an unskulled Death Game character counts as **two** unjust kills.
- Every saved death is logged; staff can review patterns.

### 6.7 Spells in PvP (Jay, 2026-11-17)
Jay: "im split between full, letting effects happen and everything be kinda chaotic but also have a system of checks
for everything, so one class isnt to powerful... on the flip side, just because it sounds fun to me doesnt mean the
tibia community will go with it." Decided with Claude: **the proven Tibia core for controlling players, Jay's
counter system as terrain, and full chaos tested as a weekly arena event first.** The idea behind it: players
accept losing a path, they hate losing control of their character; terrain counters are decisions, not reflexes, so
bots and lag can't decide them.

- **Damage:** every hit between players is halved (Tibia's rule, already in the engine; black skulls take full).
  Burn and bleed ticks are halved too, and finisher/opener bonuses (Final Verdict, Mercy Shot, Ambush Shot) give half.
- **Holding players:** roots and slows last half as long on players (at least 1 s); afterwards the player can't be
  held again for 4 s. **Haste breaks slows and roots** (Quickstep, Swift Foot, Gale Step), as in Tibia.
- **Moving players:** only the Vanguard: **Iron Reach** pulls a player next to you, **Shield Bash** knocks one back
  and roots briefly. Every other push, pull or gather (Repulse, Gravity Well, Scatterwind, Tidecrash...) moves monsters only.
- **Terrain:** the Mystic's **Vinewall** (3 vines across your path, 20 s); **fire burns vines** (every Arcanist fire
  spell, burning ground, Firewake); the Arcanist's **Barrier Rune** (a 20 s wall; only Arcanists make and use it);
  the Ranger's **Breach Arrow** breaks the first vine or barrier ahead.
- **Cheat death:** Undying Oath works against players (the Vanguard's clutch, 2-minute cooldown); Lifeseed saves from
  monsters only.
- **Wild PvP:** one arena event a week where every push, pull, slow and root works on players at full strength, no
  immunity. If the community loves it, it can graduate to a zone (Deadzone).

| Class | PvP specialty | Its counters |
|---|---|---|
| Vanguard | pulls and pushes players, gap-closers, Undying Oath, shields allies | kited; walls stop its leaps |
| Ranger | range, escapes, roots from afar, finisher, breaks walls | weak up close; pulled in by the Vanguard |
| Arcanist | strongest beams and bursts, burning ground, Barrier Rune, Tailwind (party haste that frees from slows) | fragile; can't push or trap besides the barrier |
| Mystic | heals and cures, roots, Vinewall, paralysis rune | fire burns its walls; haste breaks its roots |

## 8. Exploration

### 8.1 Secrets
Hidden shops, dungeons, quests, bosses, fishing spots, NPCs, chests and Easter eggs. Target: **12 secrets per floor** at launch (120 total) **(Claude's call)**, with a mix of practical (dungeon, shop, quest reward) and flashy/silly (Jay: he'd sell the flashy ones and keep the useful ones).

**How it works (Jay's design):**
1. Each secret has a trigger point (a tile, a lever, an NPC phrase, a fishing catch, a timed event).
2. When a player touches or passes the trigger, the discovery is recorded as **theirs** (with time and floor). Message: *"You discovered <secret>. The Info Broker would pay for this."*
3. They can **sell** it to the Info Broker NPC (**Vesper**, original name) or **keep it private**.
4. **Selling** pays gold by tier (small / medium / large × floor) and makes the secret **public**: server-wide announcement (*"<player> has sold word of <secret> on Floor 4!"*) and it appears on the broker's list with directions. The seller gets an "Informant" credit on their profile.
5. **Keeping it private never locks anyone out.** Anyone else can find it the normal way. It's simply their secret until someone sells it.
6. Once public, nobody else can sell it. Players who found it earlier keep their discovery record (it still counts for explorer titles).

**What secrets pay (Jay, 2026-11-27):** "i feel like 1-3 secrets per floor should give gear, the rest can give special cosmetics or titles. players wont know what gives what so it will be up to them to decide if the secrets worth keeping, or rolling the dice to get an exclusive item." **Jay's clarification, same day (this is the rule):** "i do not want the location of the secret announced. the only hints i want for the secrets need to be coded in thick riddles. it should be incredibly difficult to find a secret through the hints alone. this will encourage heavy wondering and exploring of the server. rewards from secrets: (always) a medium gold reward. 1-3 exclusive items total that can only be obtained through secrets per floor. the rest of the rewards can be titles, cosmetics, pets, or auras." So: every hint is a riddle with no place names or directions; nothing else in the game points at a secret; Vesper's public list shows the riddle, not directions (Claude's reading; this replaces "with directions" in step 4 above; Q99). **(Claude's calls, Jay can veto:)** secret gear is a ring, amulet or boots, never a weapon or armour, and can't be traded; rare finds are announced and visible; each season's first finders get looks and titles only; rare roaming monsters, treasure maps, a Discoveries page and rumours. Full design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-27-adventure-layer-design.md`.

**Claude's calls:** sale payout = floor base × tier (small ×1, medium ×2, large ×4). Account-wide **explorer titles** carry across seasons (10, 50, 100 discoveries; "Cartographer" for finding every secret on a floor). A secret's interior is never required for the main path (floors, labyrinths, story quests).

**Gear and weapon progression (Jay, 2026-11-23)** is designed in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-23-gear-progression-design.md`: a permanent style tree per character with two locked-in choices, weapons swapped floor by floor with tiers (tier tokens) and familiarity, a clear checklist per floor in a "Next upgrade" window, and hidden mini-bosses. ### 9.4 How monsters spawn: habits, ground types and the floor recipe (Jay, 2026-11-16)

Jay, 2026-11-16: *"i think each spawn should be a little different. there shouldn't be one uniform rule across the board for how many monsters to a spawn... the system we currently have of 3 monsters per group isnt working either. it might be alright for one monster specifically... we need to have some kind of spawn rule or way to design every floors spawns so they are similar but not the same. also keeping in mind certain classes will hunt better at certain areas. we could make a certain area for all of them to train semi decent solo. but i think the best hunting grounds should have to be hunted as a group."* He agreed to the three layers below, and added: *"we should also create an occasional hunting ground that can support 4-8 players."*

**Layer 1. Every monster has a habit.** The habit belongs to the monster and goes with it to every ground it appears in. Which monster gets which habit is **(Claude's call, Jay can veto)**.

| Habit | How it spawns | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Loner | always alone, well apart, tougher per monster | Vanguard |
| Pair | twos, sometimes a single | everyone |
| Pack | 3 to 5 that stay together, one of them a leader | groups; strong solo players |
| Swarm | 5 to 8 weak ones in a clump that die fast | Arcanist (area spells) |
| Colony | thin on the outside, thick round a nest | everyone outside, groups at the nest |
| Sentry | stands guard at fixed spots, often ranged | Ranger |

**Layer 2. Every hunting ground has a type.** The type says who the ground is for and how much danger it may hold.

| Type | Who hunts it | Reward target (per person, per hour) |
|---|---|---|
| Training ground | any class, solo, semi-decent; mostly loners and pairs | 100% (the floor's baseline) |
| Class ground | one class does clearly better; the others can hunt it, just slower | about 120% for the right class |
| Mixed ground | two or three habits side by side; fine solo, better as a pair | about 110% |
| Group ground | 3 to 4 players: mixed packs (a front-liner, a ranged one, a caster) that need a tank, damage and healing | about 150%, the floor's best |
| Large-group ground | 4 to 8 players; one per floor at most (Jay: "occasional") | about 175% |

- What makes a class ground (layout and habit together): **Vanguard**, tight tunnels and hard-hitting loners and pairs that walk up to you. **Ranger**, open ground and slow, tough monsters or sentries, with room to keep your distance. **Arcanist**, swarms. **Mystic**, long wearing fights (poison, monsters that heal or come back) where staying power and healing win. **(Claude's call.)**
- The reward targets come from the monsters and the layout, never from a hidden bonus. `tools/balance.py` holds them **(Claude's call on the numbers)**.
- One player far above a group ground's level may hunt it alone (Jay, 2026-11-16: "yes"). It must never be that player's best place to hunt.
- Party size goes from 6 to 8 so a large-group ground can be hunted in one party **(Claude's call)**.

**Layer 3. Every floor follows the same recipe, with one twist of its own.**
- Per floor, roughly: 1 or 2 training grounds, one class ground per class, 2 or 3 mixed grounds, 2 or 3 group grounds, and at most one large-group ground (usually the floor's apex spawn, 3.5).
- Each floor adds one new habit or behaviour that the floors below do not have (for example monsters that call their neighbours, or monsters that flee and come back). **(Claude's call per floor.)**
- No two grounds on a floor use the same habit in the same kind of layout.

**The danger budget replaces the head count.** Each monster has a weight: 1 for the floor's ordinary monster, less for a swarm monster, more for a loner or a leader. Each ground type has a budget for how much weight may come at a player at once, rising from the gate inward. Group and large-group grounds have budgets sized for their party. The checker (`tools/hunt_check.py`) enforces the budget of each ground's own type.

**One rule is the same everywhere:** the gate and the first stretch of every ground are quiet (Jay, 2026-11-14: no "nonstop monsters flooding to me" on walking in).

**Challenge, not calm (Jay, 2026-11-25).** *"i dont want things to be to calm. there should be challenge to the server and it to feel like a tibia ot, not apples to apples like real tibia."* And: *"spawns should be large and support a few people hunting in them at one time. certain points of the spawn can feel overwhelming (not every spawn) and force you back to the previous spawn. giving players a sense of power growth as well."* So:
- Only the gate and first stretch stay quiet. Past them, grounds are large and dense enough for a few hunters at once, with fast, OT-style respawn.
- Some grounds (not all) have an overwhelming spot: a nest, a deep room or a far end that a player at the ground's level can't hold and must back out of to the previous spawn, and comes back to once stronger.
- The danger budget is a ceiling against floods, not a target for emptiness; the numbers in `tools/balance.py` are set for challenge **(Claude's call on the numbers, Jay can veto)**.

**How monsters notice you (measured in the client, H0 and H0b, 2026-11-26).** Monsters on your floor notice you from at least 12 tiles, as soon as you come into view. A wall or corner between you does not delay them: every monster with a clear line went for the test character at once (270 of 270), and so did most behind a wall or round a corner (62 of 79). The rest (17) never came, because they had no way round to you. Monsters let go when you change level. Idle monsters (nobody to fight) drift about 3 to 5 tiles from where they stood within 40 seconds, at most 9. So map design, not wall cover, decides when a spawn wakes: a gate stays quiet only if its monsters are out of view (more than about 9 tiles across, 7 up or down) or walled off with no short way round.

## 10. Economy

- Gold / platinum / crystal coins. Gold enters from monster loot, quests, secrets sold to the broker, fishing. Gold leaves through: blessings, rent, travel fees, NPC supplies, guild/war fees, Fortune Hall cut, skull fines, repair costs **(Claude's call on sink list)**.
- Market in every town depot (Canary's built-in market).
- **Trade materials and crafting (Claude's call, Jay can veto; 2026-11-12):** every monster drops a material (honeycomb, wolf paw, turtle shell, rope belt...). NPCs pay almost nothing for materials, but town crafters turn them plus a fee into things no shop sells (sniper arrows, piercing bolts, outfit addons) or into supplies cheaper than the shop price. That is what makes hunters trade with each other. NPCs buy gear back at about a fifth of its price, so selling to players always pays more.
- **Burn-rate check:** `tools/gen_economy.py` models gold in and supplies out for every hunting ground and vocation and writes ECONOMY.md. The build fails if a vocation loses money where it is meant to hunt, or if a recipe isn't worth crafting.
- Numbers come from generated tables (`data/`, produced by `tools/gen_tables.py`), with an economy check that fails loudly if prices make no sense.

The best gear and spells come only from playing. No XP boosts, no loot boxes **(Claude's call: no XP boosts)**.

## 12. Bots and automation

Jay's rules (2026-11-08): *"Cave bot will be built in, but when it's enabled xp gain will be significantly reduced. Training manually will be much more beneficial. But it can be an option for when they afk or what not. The risk of course is being pked. Auto targeting is also going to need to be built in."*

**Background research:** Cyntara's written rules ban macros and outside programs, but its own client ships helper tools (a walker, a looter, experimental vBot support) and some rewards stay hidden from players who used the auto-walker recently. Spirebound goes further in the same direction: the helpers, cavebot included, are **built into our client**, and the game makes manual play clearly better.

**Built into the Spirebound client (allowed):**
- **Cavebot**: walks a route you record, attacks what it meets, loots, and uses supplies. Usable when you're away from the keyboard.
- **Auto-targeting**: picks the next monster for you.
- Auto-heal (spell or potion at the HP/mana % you set), auto-eat, ring/amulet refill, loot helper, hotkeys.

**The cost of the cavebot (Claude's call on numbers, Jay can veto):**
- While the cavebot is on, you get **45% of normal XP** (Jay, 2026-11-08: "You want the players to have a reason to bot occasionally") and **50% of normal loot** (Claude's call). Manual hunting is more than twice as good.
- The reduction stays for **10 minutes after you switch it off**, so nobody flicks it off just for a boss or a rare spawn (Claude's call, waiting on Jay: QUESTIONS Q11).
- No icon or marker shows who is botting (Jay). Being PKed is the risk you take (Jay); all normal PvP rules and protections still apply.
- Stamina still drains as usual.
- Secrets, rare chests and the Twin Fang trial never trigger while the cavebot is on, or within 10 minutes of using it.
- Boss rooms (field and floor bosses) refuse to let a cavebot character in.
- **Auto-targeting on its own (no cavebot) costs nothing.** It's a convenience for players at the keyboard **(Claude's call)**.

**In fights with players, the helpers only heal (Jay, 2026-11-25):** *"i think the bot should work for pvp strictly for healing. no auto hasting or auto counters to any pvp elements, strictly healing."*
- **When it applies:** from the moment a player hits you or you hit a player, until the combat lock wears off (Tibia's usual lock, about 1 minute). Against monsters everything works as normal. *(Claude's call)*
- **Still on:** auto-heal (healing spells and potions) and auto-eat.
- **Switched off:** auto-haste, auto attack spells, auto-targeting players, auto-casting clutch spells (Undying Oath, Lifeseed...), auto-breaking vines or barriers, and **automatic ring and amulet swaps** (putting on a protective amulet when a fight starts is a counter, not healing). *(the last one: Claude's call)*
- **The cavebot stops walking** when a player attacks: the character stands still and only heals until the lock ends, so it can't run away. Being PKed stays the risk of botting. *(Claude's call)*
- The server already limits how often anyone can heal, so a bot heals no faster than a person, only more reliably. If PvP tests show bot-healed characters are too hard to kill, add a short reaction delay to auto-heal during player fights.
- These limits are built into our client; a client changed to get around them falls under the hacked-client ban below.

**Not allowed:**
- Any third-party bot or modified client. Our client is the only allowed client.
- Auto-fishing (fishing has its own anti-AFK "struggle" click) **(Claude's call)**.
- Cheating the cavebot penalty (e.g. a hacked client that hides the cavebot) = immediate ban.

**Enforcement:**
- The client tells the server when the cavebot is on, and the server applies the XP/loot cut. The server also logs patterns that look like an unmarked bot (identical routes for 20+ minutes, 24-hour sessions with steady XP, impossibly fast reactions) for staff review. No automatic bans.
- Punishment for third-party bots or a hidden cavebot: first offense 7-day ban and removal of gains; second offense permanent.
- Up to **3 clients per computer** at once **(Claude's call, was 2; raised because of Jay's 2026-11-25 answer below; QUESTIONS Q53)**.
- Rules are published on the website and in game.

**Extra characters, multi-clienting and trainers (Jay, 2026-11-25):** *"i dont mind extra character, players playing multi accounts, or online trainers. that can push players in the direction of playing things they usually wouldnt otherwise. extra characters shouldnt be confided to town."* So extra characters play anywhere, with no town-only rule, and online trainers are allowed.

**Clean Count and Exile Ledger (from Grok's research R2, kept in the form Jay's answer allows):**
- The website shows **characters online and real people online side by side**, so nobody thinks the count is padded.
- Every ban for cheating (third-party bots, hacked clients, real-money trading) is **public with its reason** in the Exile Ledger on the website. A ban covers the person's other accounts too.
- Cheat detection runs on the server only (no scanning of players' computers).
- Not adopted: Grok's "one fighting character per person" rule (Jay's answer above).

**Lessons from why players quit, and our answer:**
- Pay-to-win → never (DESIGN 11).
- Short life / fear of resets → seasons are announced months ahead, account-wide rewards carry over (DESIGN 4).
- Bad first 2 hours → the guided Arrival Yard, no-loss deaths below 50, test the first 2 hours in every playtest.
- Early XP too strong, then nothing to do → stages taper and floors unlock content over the season.
- Real-money traders, launch rushers → no character bazaar; RMT bans in the rules.
- Level gaps → seasons + catch-up + level-range PvP protection.
- Account theft, shop bugs → two-factor login on the website, automated tests on everything that touches the shop.
- Multi-client inflation → the website shows real people next to characters online (Clean Count, DESIGN 12).
- Real-money prize contests → never (Jay, 2026-11-25: "never planned on this").
